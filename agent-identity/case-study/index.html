The problem

Twilio already had identity tools built for humans — ways to verify a person is who they say they are. But there was no way to safely authenticate or authorize the AI agents now acting alongside them: software making requests, and sometimes decisions, on a user's behalf, without a person watching every step. Imagine a financial tool, Owl Trade, whose end users want to connect ChatGPT to make trades for them — there was no secure way to do that yet.

Fig. 01 — the consent screen an end user sees before an agent can act on their behalf.

Agent Identity is pre-launch — currently in testing with 5 design partners ahead of a private beta in Fall 2026. Rather than invent outcomes that don't exist yet, here's what that testing is actually working to answer:

01 How much consent-screen customization partners need beyond a logo
02 What would make configuration more intuitive
03 What actions on their platform would need human-in-the-loop approval
04 What custom scopes they'd need to define
05 What problem they'd want solved next
